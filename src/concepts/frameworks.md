# Frameworks and the Zoom

**A frame is a path you hand over, so the listener does not have to build it.**

After the point, he says the audience needs a path. The purpose of a framework is not decoration.

> "They exist to reduce the cognitive tax that we place on our listener."
>
> [How To Speak Clearly (Full Course)](videos/2026-10-31-How-To-Speak-Clearly-Full-Course.md), 2026-10-31.

He defines that tax as the mental effort required to understand what you are saying. A CEO should not make employees work out the point. A salesperson should not make the prospect organize the pitch.

Three frames in that course:

- DOD. Decision, owner, deadline. What is being discussed, what decision is being made, who owns it, and the deadline.

> "the DOD, the decision owner deadline framework."
>
> [How To Speak Clearly (Full Course)](videos/2026-10-31-How-To-Speak-Clearly-Full-Course.md), 2026-10-31.

- Problem, cause, solution. His sales example: calls convert poorly, because too much time goes to answering questions instead of the prospect's problem, so they change the first 20 minutes.

> "Another very common one is problem cause solution."
>
> [How To Speak Clearly (Full Course)](videos/2026-10-31-How-To-Speak-Clearly-Full-Course.md), 2026-10-31.

- PREP. Point, reason, example, point. He says to pick the frame that fits the call. Decision-heavy talk gets DOD. Exploring an idea can use point, reason, explanation, point. He says both "example" and "explanation" in that minute. Both words are in the caption. This page does not collapse them.

> "point reason example point."
>
> [How To Speak Clearly (Full Course)](videos/2026-10-31-How-To-Speak-Clearly-Full-Course.md), 2026-10-31.

Control the zoom is the next move in the same course. A long answer when a short one was needed is what he calls an overpour, or a rambler. The question he tells people to steal:

> "How much detail do you need on this right now from 1 to 10?"
>
> [How To Speak Clearly (Full Course)](videos/2026-10-31-How-To-Speak-Clearly-Full-Course.md), 2026-10-31.

He ties that question to value per minute: high resolution is a 10, low resolution is a 1. See [Value per Minute](value-per-minute.md).

Silence has a name. On 6 July 2026, talking to engineers, he calls two or three seconds of nothing a gift.

> "the generosity pause is this idea of viewing silence, viewing two to three seconds of nothing as an offering, as a gift to your audience."
>
> [Why Smart Engineers Sound Confusing](videos/2026-07-06-Why-Smart-Engineers-Sound-Confusing.md), 2026-07-06.

On the clarity course he uses the same name for a different cue: when he couples uncommon words, he pauses so the listener can digest them. Same name, two neighboring uses. One is a gift of time. One is a pause after a new pair of words.

Stopping is its own skill. On 1 June 2026 he pairs it with a closing bookend, often "that's the idea," and he calls the decision to stop information discipline.

> "information discipline, knowing when to stop."
>
> [When Thinking Too Much Ruins Your Speaking](videos/2026-06-01-When-Thinking-Too-Much-Ruins-Your-Speaking.md), 2026-06-01.

On 26 July 2026 the mental period is a period you place after the sentence, said in the mind, so you do not keep decorating a finished line.

> "This consists of placing a mental period after each of your sentences."
>
> [How To Be More Articulate Than 99% of People](videos/2026-07-26-How-To-Be-More-Articulate-Than-99-of-People.md), 2026-07-26.

On the clarity course the same phrase is more physical. He says "period" and taps his fingers. He also offers a pressure hold between thumb and finger. Discomfort around 30 seconds is the signal to wrap up. He calls that hold an artificial clock. It is a practice he suggests in the video, not a measured protocol.

> "this creates a what we call an artificial clock."
>
> [How To Speak Clearly (Full Course)](videos/2026-10-31-How-To-Speak-Clearly-Full-Course.md), 2026-10-31.
