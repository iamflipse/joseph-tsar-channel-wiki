# Words

**He collects lines by saying them, and he draws a word on two axes.**

On 14 June 2025 the mistake is finding a phrase and trusting memory.

> "Vocabulary is more about muscle than it is memory."
>
> [How I Collect Articulate Language](videos/2025-06-14-How-I-Collect-Articulate-Language.md), 2025-06-14.

You have to try the word on. He says there is a process so the word actually shows up in speech. The page keeps that claim and does not invent the steps beyond the caption on that video.

On 17 October 2026 the one habit is a book.

> "It's called a commonplace book."
>
> [How to Become Dangerously Articulate (With One Habit)](videos/2026-10-17-How-to-Become-Dangerously-Articulate-With-One-Habit.md), 2026-10-17.

He lists Jefferson, King, Lincoln, Fry, Emerson, and Locke as people who kept words and phrases they used. He says his own book is phrases he has said and phrases he wants. The title's "dangerously" is the title. The practice in the caption is the list.

Filler words, 9 May 2023, are likes, ums, you knows, ands, buts, and extra conjunctions. He calls them buffer words, the ones you default to while the thought catches up. He says three techniques got them out of his speech, with some exceptions. The first:

> "what are often called buffer words"
>
> [How I've Banished Filler Words From My Speech](videos/2023-05-09-How-I-ve-Banished-Filler-Words-From-My-Speech.md), 2023-05-09.

> "the first technique that has made a noticeable difference is to focus on processing the endings of your words"
>
> [How I've Banished Filler Words From My Speech](videos/2023-05-09-How-I-ve-Banished-Filler-Words-From-My-Speech.md), 2023-05-09.

He says attention on the endings redirects him. A 5 June 2023 video says the same first practice in the plural, "adopting two practices," and that hearing the endings slows him down and puts his focus on the words. Two videos, one practice, a year of the channel between the short filler video and the "five secrets" video.

An articulate word, 4 May 2023, is not the same as an articulate speaker. He says he woke up with a graph, not quite an epiphany, and drew two axes.

> "this axis represents not common to Common and this axis represents how precise"
>
> [What Is An Articulate Word?](videos/2023-05-04-What-Is-An-Articulate-Word.md), 2023-05-04.

One axis runs from not common to common: how established the word is in what he calls society's lexicon. The other runs from precise to vague. A precise word is specialized to what you are describing. A vague word, in his example, is the range a politician uses when they will not commit. The caption is loose on spelling and on which quadrant is the prize. This page does not name a winning quadrant he did not name in a clean sentence.

A verbal brand, on the channel, is the 1 October 2024 video.

> "what is a verbal brand the boundaries set around the words and phrases someone will and will not use"
>
> [How to speak like the 1% elite](videos/2024-10-01-How-to-speak-like-the-1-elite.md), 2024-10-01.

The caption there is rapid and lightly punctuated. The same definition is also in a 10-minute X clip. This page stands on the 1 October 2024 video.
