# Presence

**He splits executive presence into four trainable qualities.**

The movie version, he says on 24 October 2026, is a suit in a boardroom. The version he teaches is a combination.

> "executive presence really is a combination of four core qualities that you need to develop as a communicator."
>
> [This is How I Learned Executive Presence](videos/2026-10-24-This-is-How-I-Learned-Executive-Presence.md), 2026-10-24.

> "It's composure, it's physical presence, it's authority, and it's conviction."
>
> [This is How I Learned Executive Presence](videos/2026-10-24-This-is-How-I-Learned-Executive-Presence.md), 2026-10-24.

He says these show up when pressure is high and the mind is full, which is the same setting as the clarity course. Later in that video he treats conviction as something with its own drills, including a prepared phrase for disagreement ("Let me offer some pushback to that idea") and defending what he calls conversational territory. Those are later items in the same caption, not extra qualities beyond the four.

He says he is not asking you to believe in executive presence. He is inviting you to develop it, and he says composure, physical presence, authority, and conviction can be trained.

Assertiveness, 7 February 2026, is a system he says he developed and that the coaches teach inside Elite Speak. The result he describes, from a client named Pete, is less resentment, fewer misunderstandings, and more respect, because the person can lay down a line. The opening principle:

> "just because you're not assertive doesn't mean you're weak."
>
> [How To Be Assertive (Full Course)](videos/2026-02-07-How-To-Be-Assertive-Full-Course.md), 2026-02-07.

Listening, 14 January 2026, fails in a specific way. People do not feel heard, usually not because you missed a fact.

> "It's because you missed an emotion"
>
> [How To Listen Attentively](videos/2026-01-14-How-To-Listen-Attentively.md), 2026-01-14.

He says a feeling is encoded in nearly everything people say, and that attentive listening is a skill you may simply not have been shown.

Social anxiety, 24 January 2025, is not presented as a flaw to delete. He says the most powerful person in the room does not care what others think, and that reaching that state passes through caring what they think. Socially anxious people, in this video, are intelligent, very aware, and often turning that awareness inward. He calls the awareness a sword they poke themselves with. The title says "cheat code." The caption's claim is the sword, not a guarantee of success.

Talking to higher-ups, sounding credible when you are the youngest, the two-minute reset, and the three-step routine before a high-stakes conversation are later lessons in the same neighborhood. They are separate captions in the [catalog](../videos.md). This page does not weld them into one routine he did not name.
